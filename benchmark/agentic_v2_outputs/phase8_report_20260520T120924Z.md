# Taming Telecom Turbulence: A Data-Driven Strategy to Reduce Customer Churn
**Prepared by:** Agentic AI Data Analyst Pipeline (Claude Opus)
**Date:** 20 May 2026
**Objective:** Identify the top drivers of customer churn in our telecom subscriber base and recommend the two or three highest-leverage, cost-effective interventions to reduce it.
**Audience:** Customer Retention / Marketing leadership

---

## Executive Summary
Our analysis reveals that **88.5% of all customer churn originates from customers on Month-to-Month (M2M) contracts**, despite them making up only 55% of the customer base. The root cause is a structural vulnerability: this large segment has minimal commitment and low switching costs, making them highly susceptible to churn. We recommend launching a targeted Q3 "Loyalty Upgrade" campaign to migrate high-tenure M2M customers to 1-year contracts, which could prevent over 600 customers from churning. A secondary, highly actionable finding is that customers paying by Electronic Check churn at three times the rate of those on automatic payments, indicating an urgent need to reduce payment friction.

---

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: Month-to-Month contracts are the epicentre of the churn problem.
**Evidence:**
-   Customers on M2M contracts churn at a rate of **42.7%**. This is nearly 4 times the rate of customers on a One-year contract (11.3%) and 15 times the rate of those on a Two-year contract (2.8%).
-   This segment alone accounts for **88.5% of all churned customers**. Addressing this single group is the most direct path to reducing overall churn.

**Root Cause:** The business currently allows 55% of its customer base to exist in a low-commitment state with minimal barriers to exit. This product strategy is misaligned with retention goals, creating a perpetually leaky bucket.

**Confidence:** HIGH. This finding is statistically significant with a large effect size. We are over 99% confident this is a real and powerful pattern, not random noise.

**Business Implication:** If left unaddressed, marketing efforts to acquire new customers will be continuously undermined by the high attrition rate of this foundational segment. We are effectively refilling a bucket with a large hole in the bottom.

### Finding 2: Manual "Electronic Check" payments introduce critical friction and triple the churn risk.
**Evidence:**
-   Customers paying by Electronic Check have a churn rate of **45.3%**.
-   This is dramatically higher than all automated payment methods, such as Bank transfer (**16.7%**) or Credit card (**15.2%**).

**Root Cause:** A manual, high-friction payment process leads to preventable "passive churn" from payment failures, inconvenience, or late fees. There is no proactive program to migrate these high-risk customers to more reliable, automated payment methods.

**Confidence:** HIGH. This relationship is also statistically significant with a medium-to-large effect size, confirming it as a major operational risk.

**Business Implication:** A significant portion of churn is not due to dissatisfaction with the core product, but with a solvable operational inefficiency in the billing process. This is low-hanging fruit for churn reduction.

### Finding 3: "Protective" add-on services are powerful retention tools, not just upsells.
**Evidence:**
-   Among internet customers, those lacking add-ons like **Tech Support** or **Online Security** churn at a rate of **49.0%**.
-   Customers who have at least one of these "protective" services have a churn rate of only **17.1%**, a reduction of nearly two-thirds.

**Root Cause:** The current product strategy positions these services as optional upsells rather than essential components for retaining high-value, high-expectation customers (especially those on the higher-churn Fiber Optic plan). Customers encountering technical issues without support become frustrated and leave.

**Confidence:** HIGH. The analysis confirms with over 99% confidence that these services have a strong negative association with churn.

**Business Implication:** We are missing a key opportunity to increase customer "stickiness" and demonstrate value. By not bundling these services, we fail to provide a safety net for customers, leading to preventable service-related churn.

## Anomalies & Surprises
-   **Observation:** The relationship between the number of add-on services and churn is not linear. Churn risk is low for customers with zero add-ons, *peaks* for customers with only one, and then steadily decreases as more are added.
-   **Possible Explanation:** The "zero add-ons" group includes customers without internet service, who are naturally very low-churn and skew the baseline. A single add-on may not create enough value or "stickiness" to retain a customer, but two or more signals a deeper integration into our product ecosystem.
-   **Why It Matters:** This suggests that retention strategies should focus on bundling *at least two* value-added services, rather than just one, to achieve a meaningful reduction in churn.
-   **Suggested Action:** A follow-up analysis should identify the most effective *pair* of add-on services for retention.

## Caveats & Limitations
-   **Cost-Effectiveness:** This analysis assumed the business has its own cost data to evaluate financial feasibility (A4). We have identified high-leverage interventions, but without data on implementation costs or customer lifetime value, we cannot definitively state they are the most "cost-effective." To resolve, Finance should model the ROI for each recommendation.
-   **Correlation vs. Causation:** This analysis identifies strong correlations, not proven causal links. For example, while M2M contracts are strongly associated with churn, the underlying cause may be that less-committed customers choose these plans. Our recommendations are based on the strong hypothesis that changing these factors will influence behavior, which should be confirmed via piloting.
-   **Data Representativeness:** This analysis assumed the provided dataset is a representative sample of the entire customer base (A1). If the sample is skewed (e.g., towards a specific region or time period), the generalizability of these findings may be limited.
-   **Data Cleaning:** The 11 newest customers had $0 in Total Charges, which we imputed from null. This is a logical assumption but could slightly understate the financial profile of the newest cohort if any initial pro-rated charges were expected. This has no material impact on the key findings.

## Methodology Appendix
-   **Data sources:** The analysis was performed on the IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset (Telco-Customer-Churn.csv), containing 7,043 customer records.
-   **Cleaning summary:** Key data preparation steps included: converting the `TotalCharges` column to a numeric type; imputing 11 null `TotalCharges` values with 0.0 based on their corresponding `tenure` of 0; and standardizing the `SeniorCitizen` column from 0/1 to 'No'/'Yes' for consistency.
-   **Statistical tests:** Associations between categorical variables and churn were validated using Chi-squared tests of independence. The relative importance of drivers was quantified using a Logistic Regression model. All key findings were statistically significant at a 99% confidence level.
-   **Assumptions:**
    1. The provided IBM Telco dataset is a representative sample of the entire customer base.
    2. The 'Churn' column accurately reflects customer attrition over a consistent and relevant time period.
    3. 'Drivers' of churn can be effectively proxied by identifying features with the highest statistical correlation or model-derived importance.
    4. The business has access to its own cost data to evaluate the financial feasibility of any recommended interventions.
    5. The `tenure` column is measured in a consistent unit, assumed to be months.
